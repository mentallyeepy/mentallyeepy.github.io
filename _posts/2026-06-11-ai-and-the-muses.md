AI is an amazing tool. It can guide you through writing a resume, earning quick cash, quitting a job, starting a business, and filing for bankruptcy. It can also generate images for you or generate music.\
However, despite the fact that it's amazing and sometimes treated like an all-knowing best friend, AI can also hallucinate, be inaccurate, and be prone to oversimplifying complex issues. This makes it important to examine its tall tales carefully, especially when discussing how it's changing the wonderful world of music.

For this experiment we're going to be using the one and only, infamous for being there for no reason, Microsoft Copilot.\
Here's the prompt I've used:
`Explain how artificial intelligence is changing music composition and music production.`

And here's how it responded:

---
Artificial intelligence is reshaping both music composition and music production by expanding creative possibilities, automating technical tasks, and introducing entirely new artistic workflows. At its core, AI is becoming a co‑creative partner—not just a tool—changing how artists write, arrange, produce, and even perform music.

---
# 🎼 How AI Is Changing Music Composition
## 1. AI as a Co‑Composer
   AI systems are increasingly used to generate melodies, harmonies, lyrics, and full compositions, often in collaboration with human artists. Research shows musicians use AI for AI composition, co‑composition, sound design, lyrics generation, and translation, treating AI as both a creative assistant and an artistic medium. 

## 2. Algorithmic and ML‑Driven Composition
   Machine learning models—especially deep learning—enable:
   - Algorithmic composition that learns from large corpora of music.
   - Emotion‑driven music generation, where models produce music tailored to a mood or affective state.
   - Automatic music transcription and music information retrieval, which feed into composition workflows.
   
   These tools allow composers to explore styles, structures, and ideas that would be difficult or time‑consuming to create manually.

## 3. New Artistic Formats
   AI is enabling:
   - Multilingual and multigenre song releases
   - Uncanny or experimental aesthetics
   - Online installations and interactive music experiences
   
   Artists are using AI not just to write music but to rethink what a musical work can be.

---
# 🎚️ How AI Is Changing Music Production
## 1. AI‑Enhanced Studio Workflows
   AI is beginning to transform production practices, especially in genres where studio technology is central. Current applications include:
   - AI‑based sound synthesis
   - Audio editing and mixing assistance
   - Automatic arrangement and track editing
   
   These tools integrate composition, editing, and mixing into a more fluid, iterative process. 

## 2. Audio‑Based Tools Over MIDI‑Based Tools
   Producers working with contemporary popular music often rely on audio‑based AI tools rather than symbolic (MIDI) tools because:
   - They better match real‑world studio workflows.
   - They allow manipulation of timbre, texture, and performance nuances.
   - They integrate more naturally with DAWs and recorded material. 

## 3. ML‑Assisted Production Techniques
   Machine learning is now used for:
   - Automatic mixing and mastering
   - Stem separation
   - Sound classification and tagging
   - Recommendation of production choices

   These capabilities streamline technical tasks and free producers to focus on creative decisions. 

---
# 🎵 The Bigger Picture: A Transformative Shift
Across both composition and production, AI is:
- **Lowering barriers to entry** for new creators.
- **Expanding the creative palette** for professionals.
- **Blurring the line** between human and machine creativity.
- **Raising new ethical and copyright questions**, especially around training data and authorship. 

AI’s role is still evolving, but the trend is clear: it is becoming deeply integrated into the musical process, not as a replacement for human creativity but as a powerful extension of it.

After checking out what the AI said for itself, I searched on Google on the same topic and got the above sources. Here's what AI got right, oversimplified, and forgot:

# What AI Got Right:
- AI is increasingly used in music composition and production.
- AI can generate melodies, harmonies, lyrics, and complete songs.
- AI can assist with mixing, mastering, sound design, and editing.
- AI raises ethical and copyright concerns.
- AI is generally being used as a tool alongside musicians rather than completely replacing them.

# What AI Oversimplified:
- AI is becoming a co-creative partner.\
  This isn't wrong, but really vague; it's as if everyone in the industry is using it.\
  Here's what the sources think:
  - Some artists embrace it
  - Some artists hate it
  - Many musicians are worried about copyright and originality (copilot acknowledges this but doesn't expand)
  - There is still a major uncertainty about how AI should be used
- AI isn't a replacement for human creativity.\
  This is more of an opinion than a fact.
  Source two says AI should enhance creativity rather than replace it, however many people disagree.
  A lot of musicians are worried about:
  - Job displacement
  - Reduced demand for composers
  - Synthetic voices replacing performers, for example: ACE Studio and Cantai

# What AI Forgot
- Training Data Issues\
  Copilot mentions copyright and originality issues but doesn't expand.
  Sources say:
  - AI models are trained on existing music, like Drake and The Weeknd
  - Questions exist about **artist consent**
  - Questions exist about **compensation**
  - Questions exist about **ownership**
- Impact on New Artists:\
  Source one mentions: "Many streaming platforms are integrating AI-based algorithms to deliver music to their users. This can make discovering new content overwhelming for listeners and impossible for new artists."\
  Copilot skips this despite the fact that this is a **HUGE** industry concern.
- Authenticity and Expression\
  Source one talks about whether AI can really capture human emotional nuance.
  Source two talks about formulaic and repetitive music.\
  Copilot completely disregards this.

In the end, AI is pretty neat, but users seriously have to fact-check answers, even if you're gonna use Google Gemini, which has the power of directly accessing Google search results.

## Reflection:
This assignment helped me see the good and bad of AI up close. AI helped me get quick information about how AI is being used in music composition and production. It also was a good starting point for research and provided a bunch of ideas that I could explore later.
Even after that, I also found that AI has important weaknesses. Much of the information it provided was correct, sure, but some info got oversimplified and/or lacked important context. For example, the response mentioned copyright concerns but didn't expand on that huge issue and ongoing debate on training data, artist compensation, and ownership of AI-generated music. This showed why AI information should always be verified with credible sources.
I believe that AI can be used responsibly in school and beyond when it's used as a tool, and not as a substitute for independent thinking. It's a good way for people to brainstorm, organise info, and start research, but they still have to fact-check whatever Mr. AI says and form their own conclusions.
What I'm excited about AI is the fact that it can assist creative work. As a musician into composing music, I think AI can be used to help musicians generate song/lyric ideas and experiment with new techniques. At the same time though, I worry about the way AI models are trained, and whether artists are properly credited or compensated for their work when it's used in AI training. This assignment has taught me that AI can be a very good resource but should always be used responsibly all the time.
