The bassoon is an amazing double reed instrument which is pretty confusing to put together at first, and I'm speaking from experience. So today, I'll show you how to put together your own bassoon so that you're ready to play.

---

## Step 1
When you first open your case, you're gonna notice 4 parts of the instrument and 2 mandatory accessories.
Those being the boot joint, the tenor (wing) joint, the bass joint, the bell, the seat strap, and the right hand rest.

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Picture of a bassoon case" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0005edit.webp" />

The first thing you absolutely have to do is grab that seat strap and place it on the seat where the hook/saddle are on the right hand side of the seat like so:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Seat with seat strap" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0006.webp" />

Note: be sure to wear your harness or neck strap instead when required. Also, you should start soaking your reed for about 2-3 mins around this point once you've memorized this entire sequence (don't stress it btw, this entire sequence becomes really easy once you've done it like twice).

## Step 2
The second thing you gotta do is attach the tenor joint to the boot joint:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Tenor joint and boot joint" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0007.webp" />

**IMPORTANT**: You have to line both up in a way that the two notches are aligned with each other, otherwise your pedal notes won't be able to speak

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Closeup of the tenor joint notch and boot joint notch aligned" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0009.webp" />

Also, don't forget to attach your right hand rest onto the boot joint

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Closeup of right hand rest" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0010.webp" />

## Step 3
The third step is to attach the bass joint to the boot joint and flip down the pin:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Bass joint attached to boot joint" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0011.webp" />

When you flip down the pin be sure that it locks into place with the tenor joint:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Bass joint pin locked into the tenor joint" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0013.webp" />

## Step 4
The fourth thing you have to do is attach the bell to the bass joint:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Bell attached to bass joint" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0015.webp" />

Make sure that the low B-flat key mechanism is aligned with the bell's only tone hole.

## Step 5
The fifth thing you should do if you have a seat strap is sit down on the seat strap and attach the boot joint to the seat strap:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Boot joint on seat strap hook" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0018.webp" />

Note: when using a harness or neck strap, there is a special hook just above your right thumb mechanism specifically for this situation.

## Step 6
Now the step you've all been waiting for, attach your bocal to the tenor joint and align it so that the whisper key pad can cover it's own tone hole:

<img width="500" height="auto" alt="Close up of bocal and tenor joint aligned properly" src="/Content/Bassoon/DSC_0017.webp" />

**IMPORTANT**: if you haven't already, you should now soak your reed for around 2 to 3 minutes.

And now you've finally assembled your bassoon! You're gonna play great music, trust me.

---

Now you may be wondering, what do all these reed names mean? What's the difference between medium soft and medium hard? What does Rieger 13 mean?

Well you've come to the right place because now I'm gonna tell you what these all mean:

Softness:
* Soft (or Easy): Very easy to blow with less resistance. This is highly recommended for very new players and younger players with developing embouchures.
* Medium-soft: Slightly harder but still easy to blow with some resistance. This is great for intermediate players.
* Medium: Hard to blow with more resistance. This is the most popular and great for advancing players and pro players.
* Medium-hard: Really hard to blow with even more resistance, but it also has a very rich tone. This should not be your first ever reed, as it requires a well-developed embouchure. This is great for experienced pros.
